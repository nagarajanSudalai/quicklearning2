Best Books for Grade 1–5 Maths and English

For parents of Classes 1–5 · 6 min read

A short, practical list for home: the school textbook first, then story books that build a love of reading, and a few books that make maths feel like a game.

Start with the NCERT textbook

For Classes 1–5, your child's NCERT Maths and English textbooks set out exactly what is taught each year. They are free to read and download on the NCERT website and the government's ePathshala app. Working through the end-of-chapter activities at home is the best revision there is.

English: books that make children want to read

Classes 1–3

  • StoryWeaver by Pratham Books — thousands of free illustrated stories online, sorted into reading levels 1 to 4, in English and many Indian languages. Start at level 1 and move up.
  • Panchatantra stories — short animal tales with a lesson at the end; perfect for reading aloud and retelling.
  • Tinkle — the comic magazine; comics are a great bridge for children who find long pages of text tiring.

Classes 3–5

  • Grandma's Bag of Stories by Sudha Murty — warm, funny stories told by a grandmother to her grandchildren.
  • The Blue Umbrella by Ruskin Bond — a short novel set in the hills; a good first “chapter book”.
  • Ruskin Bond's short stories for children — simple language, real places, lots of nature.

Tip: let your child choose some of the books. A book they picked themselves is a book they will finish.

Maths: books that make numbers fun

  • Your NCERT Maths textbook and its worksheets — for steady, everyday practice.
  • Puzzles to Puzzle You by Shakuntala Devi — classic number puzzles; best for Classes 4–5 and for solving together with a parent.
  • The Sir Cumference series by Cindy Neuschwander — picture-book adventures that explain shapes, measurement and fractions through stories (Classes 3–5).
  • The Number Devil by Hans Magnus Enzensberger — a story about a boy who dreams of a mischievous number devil; best for strong readers in Class 5.

How to use these at home

  • Read aloud to younger children every day, even after they can read alone.
  • Ask one question after each chapter: “What would you have done?”
  • Keep a reading log — title, date and a star rating from your child.
  • Use a library or swap books with friends; children grow out of books fast.
