Getting Ready for Term-End Assessments

For parents of Classes 1–5 · 5 min read

In the primary years, a term-end assessment is a check-up, not a race. A little steady practice and a calm routine matter far more than long revision sessions.

First, check what your school has shared

Every school sets its own assessment calendar and syllabus for Classes 1–5, so the circular or diary note from your child's teacher is the source to trust. Look for three things: the dates, the chapters included for each subject, and the format (written paper, oral, activity or project).

Make a simple list of chapters per subject and stick it on the fridge. Seeing the whole picture on one page calms both parent and child.

A two-week plan

Days 1–10: little and often

  • 20–30 minutes a day is plenty for Classes 1–3; 30–45 minutes for Classes 4–5, with a short break in between.
  • One subject, one or two chapters a day. Rotate subjects so nothing is left for the last evening.
  • Start with what your child already knows, then move to the tricky part. Early success keeps motivation up.
  • Say it out loud. Ask your child to explain a sum or retell a story to you — explaining is the best revision there is.

Days 11–14: practise the format

  • Do one short practice sheet per subject, in the same style as the real paper.
  • Mark it together and go back only to the questions that went wrong.
  • Read the questions slowly. Many mistakes in the primary years come from rushing, not from not knowing.

Subject by subject

  • Maths: times tables, number facts and word problems. Ask “What is the question asking?” before your child starts calculating.
  • English: read one short passage aloud every day and ask two questions about it. Practise spellings by writing, not just reading.
  • EVS / Science: use pictures — draw the parts of a plant, sort things into living and non-living, talk about the water cycle while boiling water.
  • Hindi or the second language: short dictation of five words a day works better than one long session.

The night before and the morning of

  • No new topics the night before. A light look at the chapter list, then early to bed.
  • Pack the bag the evening before: pencils, eraser, sharpener, scale, water bottle.
  • A proper breakfast and a relaxed morning. Leave a few minutes early.
  • Last words matter: “Do your best and read each question slowly” beats “Don't make mistakes”.

After the assessment

Talk about what felt easy and what felt hard — then let it go. When the paper comes home, look at the mistakes together as clues for what to practise next, not as a score to worry about.
