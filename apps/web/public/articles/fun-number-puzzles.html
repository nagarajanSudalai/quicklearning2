7 Fun Number Puzzles for Primary Students

For Classes 1–5 · 6 min read

Puzzles turn practice into play. Try one a day at the dinner table — answers are hidden under each puzzle so you can check together.

1. Missing number (Classes 1–2)

What comes next? 2, 4, 6, 8, __ And here? 5, 10, 15, __

Answer

10 (count in twos) and 20 (count in fives).

2. Number pyramid (Classes 1–3)

Each block is the sum of the two blocks below it. The bottom row is 2, 3, 4. What is at the top?

Answer

The middle row is 2 + 3 = 5 and 3 + 4 = 7. The top is 5 + 7 = 12.

3. Coin puzzle (Classes 2–3)

You have ₹1, ₹2 and ₹5 coins. What is the smallest number of coins that makes ₹13?

Answer

4 coins: ₹5 + ₹5 + ₹2 + ₹1.

4. Magic square (Classes 3–4)

Fill in the empty squares with 1 to 9 so that every row, column and diagonal adds up to 15.

8
6
5
4
2
Answer

Top row 8, 1, 6 · middle row 3, 5, 7 · bottom row 4, 9, 2.

5. Legs on the farm (Classes 3–5)

A farm has hens and cows. There are 10 heads and 28 legs. How many cows are there?

Answer

If all 10 were hens there would be 20 legs. Each cow adds 2 more legs, and we need 8 more, so there are 4 cows (and 6 hens).

6. Handshakes (Classes 4–5)

5 friends meet, and each shakes hands once with every other friend. How many handshakes?

Answer

The first friend shakes 4 hands, the next 3 new ones, then 2, then 1: 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10.

7. The snail and the wall (Classes 4–5)

A snail climbs a 10-metre wall. Each day it climbs 3 metres; each night it slides back 2 metres. On which day does it reach the top?

Answer

After 7 days and nights it is at 7 metres. On day 8 it climbs 3 more and reaches 10 metres — day 8, before it can slide back.

Tips for parents

  • Let your child guess first, even if the guess is wrong. Then ask “How could we check?”
  • Use real things — coins, buttons, rotis — for the younger classes.
  • Praise the thinking, not just the answer: “I like how you tried the twos first.”
